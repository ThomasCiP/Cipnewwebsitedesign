Her palette

Four jobs, four groups. Neutrals carry the outfit, heroes sit near the face, anchors hold the bottom half, and the avoid list is the one that saves the most money.

Metals: light gold, rose gold and bronze sit with auburn hair and a warm tan. Brushed or matte silver is fine; high-polish chrome and gunmetal go grey against her. Denim: mid and light blue with a clean, warm wash — not black, not inky raw indigo.

How to pair the colours

Six formulas. Each one is a repeatable structure, not a single outfit — learn these and most of the wardrobe combines itself.

The one hard rule: keep the lightness gap moderate. Her colours are light and clear, so a light-clear colour next to a deep-muted one (coral with charcoal, apricot with black) makes the dark half look like a hole and drains her face. If you want contrast, get it from hue — coral against aqua — not from light against near-black.

Shape rules

Fuller thighs and ankles with a conservative brief point to the same answer, which is convenient: longer, straighter, matte below the waist, and everything interesting up top. The goal is never to hide the leg — it is to stop cutting it into pieces.

Measured on a 170 cm frame, from the natural waist unless stated.

Legs, ankles and shoes

This is where the most damage gets done, and it is almost entirely about horizontal lines. Every strap, cuff, buckle or colour change at the ankle acts like a full stop — the eye reads the leg as ending there and being as wide as the widest thing it can see.

The shoe colour trick. A shoe close to her own skin tone reads as more leg. For a mild warm tan that is a soft camel-beige around #D6B79A — not pink-beige, not grey-beige. The alternative that works just as well: match the shoe to the trouser or the tights so the column runs unbroken from waist to floor.

The 6-point test

The rubric the fitting room scores against, and a quick check to run in a changing room. Four or more passes and it's a yes; a fail on 3 or 5 is almost always a no regardless of the rest.
